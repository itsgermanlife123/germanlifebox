02 · Fit check

Is university
right for you?

Neither path is "better". They're for different people. Be honest with yourself about which one actually fits.

Suitable if you…
  • Want a globally recognised academic degree
  • Are aiming for research, specialist, or technical roles
  • Want tuition-free (or low-cost) education at a public university
  • Prefer structured academic learning over on-the-job training
  • Are considering a field that specifically requires a degree
Not suitable if you…
  • Want to start earning a salary immediately
  • Don't want to spend 2–5 years before working full-time
  • Prefer hands-on, practical training over lectures
  • Don't meet direct-entry requirements and don't want to do a foundation year

03 · Getting ready

What you need
before you apply.

Academic qualification

Bachelor's: from Winter Semester 2026/27, Class XII needs at least 70% of the maximum marks (all boards). With that plus your APS certificate you go through a Studienkolleg foundation year; for direct subject-restricted entry you also need one successful year of a recognised Bachelor's. Other school systems (IB, A-levels) and special cases are assessed individually: check the DAAD admission database ↗. Master's: a completed, relevant Bachelor's degree.

APS Certificate

Required for most Indian applicants to verify academic credentials. APS India's updated Bachelor's rules (announced 23 Feb 2026) apply from Winter Semester 2026/27; applications submitted before 15 March 2026 aren't affected. APS checklist →

dMAT: Master's applicants, from Summer Semester 2027

If your previous degree is in Engineering, Commerce / Accounting / Finance / Economics, or Business / Management, the Digital Master Test is a required part of your APS documents from the Summer Semester 2027 intake. It's run by g.a.s.t. (not APS India) at test centres: €150, 3.5 hours, valid indefinitely, retakes allowed. No minimum score is needed for APS or the visa, though RWTH Aachen and Göttingen use the score in selection. Not needed if you registered for APS online or shipped your documents before 29 June 2026, or already hold an APS certificate. Next test: planned for early 2027. APS India: dMAT ↗ · Our dMAT guide →

Financial proof

A blocked account with €11,904/year (2026 figure, ≈€992/month) is mandatory before your visa appointment.

Age

No official upper age limit for university admission.

Health insurance

Mandatory from day one. Public student insurance costs about €141–146/month in 2026 (TK rate, including long-term care), available until age 30.

German-taught programmes

B1 is the realistic minimum, B2 is recommended, and some subjects (or Studienkolleg tracks) expect C1. Proven via TestDaF, DSH, or Goethe certificates.

English-taught programmes

IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL 80–90+ is typical. A growing number of Master's programmes, especially in STEM and Business, are fully English-taught.

Studienkolleg

Even if your target degree is English-taught, Studienkolleg foundation courses are often partly taught in German, so B1 German is usually still expected.

04 · Step by step

Your route,
in order.

Most people get confused because they don't know what comes first. Bachelor's and Master's differ in a couple of key steps, so pick yours.

Plan it month by month →
  1. 01
    SpracheLanguage

    Reach German B1–B2 (or check if you need it)

    Most German-taught programmes require B1 or B2 German. English-taught programmes need IELTS/TOEFL instead. Know your target first, then learn accordingly.

    German learning roadmap →
  2. 02
    Studienkolleg?Direct entry or foundation year

    Check your route before anything else

    From Winter Semester 2026/27, Indian Class XII with at least 70% of the maximum marks (plus APS) gives you the Studienkolleg route: a foundation year ending in a qualifying exam, the Feststellungsprüfung. Direct subject-restricted entry needs 70% in Class XII plus one successful year of a recognised Bachelor's. Check your case in the DAAD admission database before you shortlist universities, not after.

    DAAD admission database ↗APS India: 2026 update ↗
  3. 03
    APSStart early

    Get your APS certificate. This takes time.

    APS (Akademische Prüfstelle) verifies Indian academic credentials for German university applications. APS India gives no fixed processing time: it depends on how many applications it has and how quickly your school, board or university replies. So start well before your deadlines.

    APS document checklist →
  4. 04
    BewerbungUniversity selection & application

    Choose universities that match your profile

    Germany has 400+ universities. Match your CGPA, German level, and field to the right ones instead of applying everywhere. Many use Uni-Assist, where the deadline is often July 15 for the Winter semester and January 15 for the Summer semester; many universities set earlier deadlines.

    University application checklist →
  5. 05
    SOPStatement of Purpose

    Write your Statement of Purpose

    Your SOP is the most important document in your application: why Germany, why this university, why this programme, and what makes you a serious candidate. A weak SOP loses admission even with a great CGPA.

  6. 06
    SperrkontoBlocked account

    Open your blocked account (€11,904 required)

    Germany requires proof you can support yourself. A blocked account with at least €11,904 (2026 figure) is mandatory for your student visa. Expatrio and Fintiba are the most common providers. This must happen before your visa appointment.

    View recommended providers →What it all costs in ₹ →
  7. 07
    VisumApply online

    Apply for your German student visa

    Most student visas are applied for online through the Consular Services Portal (CSP), handled by the German mission for your state of residence: the Embassy in New Delhi or the Consulates General in Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata or Bengaluru. Typical documents: admission letter, APS certificate, proof of finances (blocked account), health insurance, language certificate, passport and photos. The fee is ₹8,400 (€75). Waiting times vary by mission and season, so apply as soon as you have your admission.

    Student visa checklist →
  8. 08
    AbflugBefore you fly

    Health insurance, accommodation & packing

    You need health insurance before you arrive. TK (Techniker Krankenkasse) and AOK are popular public options. Book student accommodation early; German university housing has waiting lists. Bring enough cash/forex for the first month.

    DanachAfter graduation

    Your 18-month job-search window

    After graduating, you get an 18-month residence permit (§20 AufenthG) to find a job matching your qualification. Once employed, you move to a work visa or EU Blue Card (from €45,934 gross/year in 2026 for recent graduates and shortage occupations; €50,700 otherwise; these thresholds are re-set every January). After around 2 years of qualified work, permanent residency is realistic, much faster than the standard 5-year route.

05 · Money

Tuition-free.
Not cost-free.

Public university tuition is free in 15 of 16 German states. Baden-Württemberg is the exception, charging non-EU students roughly €1,500/semester. Everywhere else, budget for this:

Semester contribution€100–350

Per semester, public universities. Covers admin and often a transport ticket.

Blocked account€11,904/yr

≈€992/month, mandatory for your visa.

Monthly living costs€842avg

What the average student in Germany spends (DAAD). Plan more for Munich, Frankfurt or Hamburg.

Working while you study

International students can work part-time to offset costs: up to 140 full days or 280 half-days per year (roughly 20 hrs/week during term, full-time during breaks), at the 2026 minimum wage of €13.90/hour. Public student health insurance costs about €141–146/month in 2026.

Scholarships

DAAD scholarships for Master's students pay €992/month; they and other scholarships are worth checking early, particularly for Winter-semester-start programmes. They can meaningfully offset the blocked-account burden if you qualify.

06 · Once you're ready

The application
playbook.

How applications actually work, when things are due, and what trips people up.

Many German universities use uni-assist.de, a centralised service that pre-checks international applications on behalf of its member universities; you submit documents once through their portal. Others, including several TU9 technical universities, accept applications directly through their own portal instead. Check each university's admission page before assuming which route applies. Using the wrong one costs you weeks.

Wintersemester

Starts October. Uni-Assist deadline is often July 15, but many Master's programmes close earlier: some Heidelberg programmes on 15 March, many TUM programmes on 31 May.

Sommersemester

Starts April, mostly Master's intakes. Uni-Assist deadline is often January 15.

Submit weeks before the deadline, not on it. uni-assist recommends applying at least 8 weeks before the deadline (its checks take about 4–6 weeks), and late or incomplete submissions are rejected outright.

  • Assuming 12th grade is enough. From Winter Semester 2026/27 you need at least 70% in Class XII, and 12th grade alone leads to Studienkolleg, not direct Bachelor's entry. Check your route before shortlisting universities, not after.
  • Starting APS too late. APS has no fixed processing time. Starting it a month before your deadline is one of the most common, most avoidable mistakes.
  • Forgetting the dMAT. Master's applicants in Engineering, Commerce / Finance / Economics or Business / Management need it from Summer Semester 2027. Test dates are few, so book early.
  • A generic SOP. A copy-pasted SOP is obvious to admissions committees who read hundreds. Be specific about this university, this programme.
  • Applying to too few, or mismatched, universities. Match your CGPA and profile realistically. Applying only to the most competitive universities is a common way to end up with zero offers.
  • Submitting on the deadline. Uni-Assist and university portals can take days to process a submission. Submitting on the exact deadline day risks missing it entirely.
  • Not budgeting the blocked-account timeline. Opening and funding a blocked account takes time to arrange. Leaving it until right before your visa appointment causes unnecessary delays.

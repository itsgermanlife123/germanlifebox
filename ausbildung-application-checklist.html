Ausbildung Application Checklist

Everything you need before you click Bewerben — the three core documents, what international applicants actually need, and the things people waste money preparing far too early.

Start here

The core package

Found an Ausbildung you're interested in? Before you click Bewerben, make sure your application is complete, targeted and submitted exactly as the employer asks. For most Ausbildung applications, the core package is:

Anschreiben+ Lebenslauf+ Zeugnisse

Depending on the occupation, the employer and your background, you may also need additional documents. Federal Employment Agency — Bewerbungsunterlagen ↗

📌 There is no universal document list

Always open the specific vacancy first. The employer's or training provider's own instructions override every generic checklist — including this one. What follows is what to check, not a list to send blindly.

Timing matters more than most applicants realise. The Federal Employment Agency notes that larger companies start looking for candidates around eighteen months before training begins. Schools and smaller companies start later, but generally by the beginning of your final school year — and banks, insurers and the public sector recruit earlier still. If you're reading this six weeks before an August start, you're late for this cycle, not for good.

Part 1

Your three core documents

✉️ Anschreiben — the cover letter

Your Anschreiben explains why you're applying for this Ausbildung at this company, and why you're a suitable candidate. The Federal Employment Agency is explicit that you should tailor it to each Ausbildungsbetrieb rather than sending the same letter everywhere — and that the same applies to your CV.

A good Anschreiben answers three questions: Why this Ausbildung? Why this company? Why you? It does not simply restate your CV in paragraphs.

📸 Do you need a photo?

No — it's optional. Current Federal Employment Agency guidance states that a Bewerbungsfoto is no longer mandatory, though it remains common in many sectors and many German recruiters still expect to see one.

If you include one: use a proper professional application photo. Not a cropped holiday snap, not a selfie, and not your passport or visa photo. A bad photo is worse than no photo. BA — building the Lebenslauf ↗

🎓 Zeugnisse — school certificates

Your certificates are the evidence behind everything you've claimed. For an Ausbildung application this normally means your relevant school certificate or certificates.

  • Most recent school certificate
  • Final school-leaving certificate, if you already have it
  • Earlier certificates, if requested — or if a previous year was stronger than your most recent one
  • Relevant Praktikumsbescheinigungen (internship certificates)
  • Relevant qualification and competition certificates
🚨 Never send originals

Keep every original document. Applications use scans or copies unless an employer specifically requests otherwise — the Federal Employment Agency advises this explicitly. Originals get lost, and you will need them later for the visa stage.

If your final certificate isn't available yet, use your most recent available school report and follow the employer's instructions.

Part 2

If you're applying from outside Germany

Applying with a foreign education brings extra questions — and this is where applicants most often spend money they didn't need to spend.

🌍Foreign school certificatesCheck first
Check what the employer or training provider actually wants: the original-language certificate, a German translation, a certified translation, recognition or equivalence documentation — or, quite often, just a scan at the initial application stage.

Do not assume every employer wants all of these. Certified translations and formal recognition cost real money. Confirm the requirement before you pay for either.
🇩🇪German language certificateConditional
Include it where the vacancy asks for it, where the training provider requires proof, where the occupation carries specific language requirements, or simply where it strengthens your case as an international applicant.

But there is no universal rule that every Ausbildung application must contain one. Requirements range from employers assessing your German themselves to formal certificate requirements in regulated fields. Building your German →
🛂Passport copyOnly if asked
A passport is obviously essential for relocation and immigration later — but it is not one of the three standard application documents. Include a copy only where the employer, training provider or portal specifically requests it.

Don't send sensitive identity documents unprompted just because you're applying from abroad. It doesn't strengthen your application, and it hands your passport data to a company that didn't ask for it.
📎Additional documentsIf relevant
Work certificates (Arbeitszeugnisse), vocational certificates, technical or language certificates, competition and volunteering certificates, work samples (Arbeitsproben), a portfolio, or a separate Motivationsschreiben where requested.

Don't attach everything you own. Ask one question of each document: does this help prove I'm suitable for this Ausbildung? If not, leave it out unless the employer asked for it.
⚠️ Two special cases worth flagging

Healthcare and nursing. These applications can require substantially more documentation than a commercial or technical Ausbildung — specific German-language requirements, translated certificates, recognition documentation, health-related documents and occupation-specific conditions. Don't apply a generic checklist to a regulated healthcare profession.

If you already hold a foreign vocational qualification. Don't assume it's treated like a school certificate. Recognition (Anerkennung) of a professional qualification is a separate procedure from a ZAB assessment of a higher-education qualification — and both are separate from school-certificate recognition. The Eligibility Checklist explains which is which →

Also note what the vacancy says about the required documents themselves — CV, cover letter, certificates, language certificate, photo, ID, portfolio, an additional questionnaire. And check whether there's a stated deadline (Einsendeschluss) and a named contact person; if there is, write down the name and contact details.

Some large employers don't want a conventional application at all. A number of major companies run structured online processes where the traditional cover-letter-plus-CV package isn't required in that form — you answer questions in a portal instead. Following your own checklist rather than theirs looks like you didn't read the posting.

Part 5

How to submit

Online portal

Follow the portal step by step. You'll typically create an account, enter personal details, upload documents individually, answer questions, and re-enter information that's already in your CV. It's tedious; do it anyway, and do it accurately.

  • Every mandatory field completed
  • Correct documents uploaded — no duplicates, no wrong files
  • Whole application reviewed before final submission
  • Confirmation page or email saved

Email

If the employer accepts email applications, use a clear subject line:

Bewerbung um eine Ausbildung als [Ausbildungsberuf] – [Your Name]

Where no specific structure is requested, one clearly organised PDF containing your core documents is the practical approach — the Federal Employment Agency describes combining the documents into a single PDF as the norm for online applications sent by email.

  • Correct recipient address
  • Professional subject line
  • Short, polite covering message in the email body
  • Correct attachment, which you have opened and checked
  • File not too large
  • Professional sender email address — not one from school
  • Copy sent to yourself as a record of submission
Part 6

After you apply

The process doesn't end when you click submit.

Track every application

The Federal Employment Agency recommends keeping an overview of where and when you applied, who the contact people are, and whether you've been invited to interview. A simple spreadsheet is enough:

Confirmation of receipt

If you get an automatic confirmation, save it. If you don't get one, don't assume anything went wrong — plenty of employers simply don't send them.

Following up

Don't chase immediately. The Federal Employment Agency puts it plainly: asking too quickly usually achieves nothing. If you've heard nothing after around four weeks, a polite enquiry about whether a decision has been made — or when you can expect one — is reasonable and shows continued interest.

Four weeks is practical guidance, not a legal deadline. If the vacancy stated its own timeline, that one wins. BA — the Ausbildung application process ↗
Part 7

What you don't need yet

This may be the most useful section on the page. These documents belong to later stages — mostly immigration — or to specific occupations and individual cases. They are usually not part of an initial employer application:

  • Blocked account (Sperrkonto)
  • Proof of financing
  • Visa application or residence permit
  • Health-insurance registration for the visa
  • A signed Ausbildungsvertrag — that comes after you're selected
  • Chamber registration confirmation
  • Visa-format biometric photographs
  • Police clearance certificate, in the general case
  • A ZAB Zeugnisbewertung, simply because you hold a foreign school certificate
  • An apostille or legalisation, solely because you're applying for an Ausbildung
💸 Don't buy visa documents before you have a training place

Certified translations, formal recognition procedures and apostilles cost money and take time. Applicants routinely spend on all three before they have a single offer — and some of it turns out to have been unnecessary for the occupation they end up in.

But the moment you receive an offer, check the next stage immediately. Some of these documents take weeks or months to obtain, and that's when the timeline gets tight. Ausbildung Eligibility Checklist → covers what the visa stage actually needs.

Avoid these

The mistakes that sink applications

  • ✕Sending the same Anschreiben everywhere. The BA specifically recommends adapting the application to each Ausbildungsbetrieb — and recruiters spot a recycled letter instantly.
  • ✕Sending documents nobody asked for. More documents do not mean a stronger application. They mean a longer one.
  • ✕Sending originals. Keep them. You'll need them at the visa stage.
  • ✕Assuming a passport copy is always required. Check the vacancy.
  • ✕Assuming a German certificate is always required. It depends on the employer, occupation and training provider.
  • ✕Ordering a ZAB evaluation you don't need. Check whether it even applies to your qualification first.
  • ✕Ignoring the portal instructions. A perfect application sent the wrong way still fails.
  • ✕Applying too late. Larger companies recruit around eighteen months ahead. By spring, many places for that autumn are gone.
  • ✕Sending unreadable scans. If a certificate is hard to read, it doesn't count as evidence.
  • ✕Losing track of applications. Where, when, who contacted you, and when to follow up — write it down or you'll lose it.

Common questions

Frequently asked

What documents do I need?+
Anschreiben, Lebenslauf and Zeugnisse. Anything beyond that depends on the employer, occupation and your circumstances — and the vacancy's instructions always take priority over a generic list.
Do I need a German CV?+
The employer's instructions decide the language. For an international applicant targeting a German-language Ausbildung, a German tabular CV is the sensible practical choice — the training itself will be in German.
Do I need an Anschreiben every time?+
Usually yes, unless the employer's process says otherwise. Some large online systems replace the cover letter with structured questions. Check before assuming.
Do I need a photo on my CV?+
No — it's optional. The BA states a Bewerbungsfoto is no longer mandatory, though it's still common in many sectors. If you include one, make it a proper professional application photo.
Do I need to translate my certificates?+
Possibly, but not automatically. It depends on the employer, training provider, occupation and stage. Check before paying for a certified translation.
Do I need a passport copy?+
Not for every application. It isn't one of the three standard documents. Include it only where it's specifically requested — don't send identity documents unprompted.
Do I need an apostille?+
Not normally, just to submit an initial employer application. Apostille and legalisation requirements can arise later for immigration or administrative procedures, and vary by country and document.
Do I need a blocked account?+
No. A blocked account relates to proving financial means for immigration, not to the employer application. Don't spend money on visa-stage documents before you have a training place.
One PDF or separate files?+
For email, one clearly ordered PDF is standard unless the employer says otherwise. For an online portal, follow its upload instructions — portals usually want documents uploaded individually.
When should I follow up?+
If you've heard nothing after around four weeks, a polite status enquiry is reasonable per BA guidance. Chasing sooner than that usually achieves nothing.
When should I apply?+
Earlier than you'd think. Larger companies look for candidates around eighteen months before training starts; schools and smaller firms start later, but generally by the beginning of your final school year. Banks, insurers and the public sector recruit earliest of all.
Do I need my Ausbildungsvertrag first?+
No. The training contract is issued after you're selected, and becomes relevant to the steps that follow — including immigration, where that applies to you.
General application guidance, not legal advice. Ausbildung application requirements vary by employer, occupation, training provider and, in some cases, federal state. The vacancy or training provider's own instructions always take priority over anything on this page.

Immigration, recognition and visa requirements are separate processes and can vary according to your nationality and circumstances. Always verify current requirements with the relevant employer, training provider and official German authorities.

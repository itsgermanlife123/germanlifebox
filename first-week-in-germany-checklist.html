You don't need to do everything on day one

You've landed, and the to-do list can feel endless. It isn't: most of it doesn't need to happen this week, and some of it doesn't need to happen at all until later. This checklist focuses on what actually matters most right now, roughly in order.

📌 Your exact list depends on your situation

What applies to you depends on your nationality, accommodation, university or training programme, and visa/residence status. Where a step is conditional, we've marked it clearly. Treat your university, your Bürgeramt and your Ausländerbehörde as the final authority for your case: this page is a practical planning tool, not a substitute for any of them.

Zuerst
03 · Priority

Check your Anmeldung

Anmeldung means registering your residence with the local registration authority (Bürgeramt or Einwohnermeldeamt). If you've moved into accommodation in Germany, you generally have two weeks from your move-in date, not your arrival date, to register.

Your two weeksSet your move-in date on the board at the top of the page to see your real dates.
    • Found your local Bürgeramt
    • Checked whether you need an appointment
    • Checked whether online registration is available
    • Prepared your passport/ID
    • Prepared your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung
    • Prepared any city-specific form
    • Completed registration within the deadline
    • Kept your registration confirmation (Meldebescheinigung)
    📌 The clock starts at move-in

    Under §17 of the Bundesmeldegesetz (BMG), the two-week clock starts the day you actually move into your accommodation, counted in calendar days, so weekends count too. Registering late is an administrative offence, so don't let it slip.

    🏠 Your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung

    Written confirmation from your housing provider that you've moved in: a rental contract alone isn't accepted at the registration office. Under §19 BMG, your housing provider has a legal duty to cooperate and issue it within the same two-week window.

    If your housing provider doesn't provide it in time, contact your local Bürgeramt directly rather than letting the deadline pass: don't just wait it out.

    04 · Priority

    Confirm your health insurance

    Health insurance matters for more than healthcare: it's also required for university enrolment.

    Your insurerM10 · electronicYour university
    • Confirmed your insurance is active
    • Checked your university has received the required notification
    • Asked your insurer if anything's still missing
    • Saved your insurance documents
    • Kept your insurance card/details accessible

    For most students, the insurer transmits confirmation electronically, directly to the university: commonly referred to as the M10 notification. Confirm with your specific insurer that it's actually been sent, rather than assuming it happened on its own.

    ⚕️ If you have private insurance

    Don't assume your policy automatically satisfies your university's requirement. Check whether you need a formal exemption from compulsory statutory student insurance (Befreiung von der Versicherungspflicht), and understand what that decision means before you proceed with it.

    2. Paid the Semesterbeitrag by the deadline
    3. Submitted any outstanding documents
    4. Confirmed your enrolment status
    5. Downloaded your Immatrikulationsbescheinigung

    Don't assume every university works the same way. Exact documents, fees, deadlines and onboarding steps are university-specific: your enrolment portal always takes priority over this checklist. More on the full journey: Study in Germany →

    Residence permit

    This section applies depending on your nationality and current immigration status: EU/EEA and Swiss citizens generally don't need this step. If you need a German residence title (Aufenthaltstitel):

    1. Register your addressAnmeldung first
    2. Contact your AusländerbehördeCheck its current procedure & document list
    3. Apply in good timeBefore your visa's validity runs out
    • Checked the validity and conditions of your current visa
    • Identified your responsible Ausländerbehörde
    • Checked its current application procedure
    • Requested/booked an appointment, where required

    Achtung
    12 · Learn from others

    Common mistakes

    • Counting the Anmeldung deadline from arrival, not move-in. These aren't always the same date.
    • Assuming a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung delay excuses a late registration. Contact the Bürgeramt instead of waiting it out.
    • Assuming private insurance automatically satisfies your university. Check whether an exemption is actually needed.
    • Trying to obtain your Steuer-ID manually in week one. It arrives automatically by post: chasing it early wastes time.
    • Leaving your residence-permit appointment until your visa is about to expire. Book it early: slots fill up fast in bigger cities.
    • Assuming every blocked-account provider releases funds the same way. Check your specific provider's process.

    13 · 💡 From experience

    Bibin's advice

    My first week, I made the mistake of trying to solve everything at once: bank account, SIM, Anmeldung, exploring the city, all in the first two days. I was exhausted and hadn't actually made progress on the one thing with a real deadline: registering my address.

    What I'd tell myself now: sort Anmeldung and your Wohnungsgeberbestätigung first, because almost everything else, the bank account, sometimes even the SIM, quietly depends on having that done. Everything else can wait a few more days.

Blocked Account
Required for your student visa — proof of financial means (€11,904+ for 2026)
Expatrio
Recommended

One of the most popular blocked account providers for Indian students. Fast setup, clear process, and accepted by all German consulates. Also offers health insurance bundles.

Bibin's note: "Setup is straightforward and their support is responsive — useful when you're doing this the first time."

Health Insurance
Mandatory in Germany — you need coverage before you arrive
TK (Techniker Krankenkasse)
Popular choice

One of Germany's largest public health insurers. Excellent English support, strong digital experience, and widely accepted. Most international students choose TK for its ease of enrollment.

Bibin's note: "TK is what most Indian students in my community go with. English support makes a real difference when you first arrive."
Visit TK →
AOK
Solid option

One of the cheapest public insurers in Germany, backed by a big regional structure built over 140+ years. Runs a fully English website and English-language hotline, so support doesn't get lost in translation.

Bibin's note: "Low monthly rates and genuinely good English support make AOK worth a look. It's organised regionally, so just confirm your specific AOK (e.g. AOK Bayern, AOK Nordost) covers your city before signing up."
Visit AOK →

Forex & International Cards
Move money from India and spend in euros without losing 3–5% to markup fees
Niyo Global
Zero forex markup

An India-based forex debit card built for students — load it in INR from any Indian bank account and spend in euros at the live Visa rate, with no markup fee. Also works as a linked, interest-earning savings account back in India.

Bibin's note: "This is what most students in the community use to avoid the 3–5% markup that regular Indian debit cards charge abroad. Order it before you fly — it takes a few days to arrive."
Get Niyo Global →
Wise
Multi-currency

A multi-currency account and card that lets you hold and spend in 40+ currencies at the real mid-market exchange rate, including euros once you're in Germany. Useful for receiving money from home or paying in other currencies while travelling.

Bibin's note: "Good as a second card for travel around Europe, but you'll still want a proper German bank account like N26 for rent and local direct debits."

3 Best SIM Cards for International Students in Germany
Vodafone
Major network

One of Germany's three major network operators, with excellent nationwide coverage and 5G in most cities. Prepaid CallYa plans work well for students who want reliable, contract-free connectivity from day one.

Bibin's note: "Vodafone costs a bit more than the discount brands, but the coverage is noticeably better if you're outside a major city."
Visit Vodafone →
sim.de
Most data per euro

A low-cost SIM-only provider run by Drillisch Online (United Internet), using the 1&1 5G network. Its Allnet Flat plans include unlimited calls and SMS within Germany, EU roaming and free number transfer, and the SIM-only tariffs can be cancelled monthly with no minimum term. The eSIM is free and activates in minutes; a physical SIM is a one-off €2.99.

Bibin's note: "You get far more data per euro here than from the supermarket prepaid SIMs — but this is a contract, not prepaid, so you'll need a German bank account for the direct debit. Start with a prepaid SIM in your first weeks, then move to sim.de once your account is open."
See sim.de plans →
O2
Good value

O2 (Telefónica) is Germany's third major network, strong in cities with competitive prepaid data plans. A solid direct-from-carrier option if you want more data per euro than Vodafone or Telekom.

Bibin's note: "O2's own coverage is weaker in rural areas, but if you're studying in a city, it's usually the cheapest of the three major networks."
Visit O2 →
ALDI TALK
Budget option

A prepaid SIM sold in ALDI supermarkets, running on the O2 network. One of the most popular budget options among students — affordable 4-week data plans with no contract or German bank account required.

Bibin's note: "This is what a lot of students in the community use for their first German number — easy to find in any ALDI, and simple to top up."

Banking in Germany
Open a German bank account — required for rent, salary, and everyday life
N26
Easy to open

German online bank with a full English app. One of the easiest accounts to open as an international student — no Anmeldung needed, so you can open it as soon as you land. Free tier available.

Bibin's note: "N26 is what I'd recommend opening first — you'll need a German account for rent and almost everything else. One thing to know: you need to be in Germany to apply, so it's a first-week task, not something to set up from India."
Open N26 account →
Sparkasse / Deutsche Bank
Traditional bank

If you need a traditional German bank with physical branches — useful for some landlords and employers who prefer established banks for direct debit mandates.

Bibin's note: "Some landlords specifically ask for a Sparkasse account. Good to have alongside an N26 as a backup."
Visit Sparkasse →

Investments & Savings
Start building while you study — even small amounts compound over time
Trade Republic
Popular in Germany

Germany's leading investment app — ETFs, stocks, and savings plans with very low fees. Widely used by Indian students in Germany who want to start investing with small amounts.

Bibin's note: "Even €25/month into a broad ETF savings plan adds up fast over a 2-year Master's. Trade Republic makes this very easy."
Open Trade Republic →
Scalable Capital
Low-cost broker

A Munich-based, BaFin-regulated investment app and bank offering ETF savings plans from as little as €1/month with no account fees on the free plan. A strong low-cost alternative to Trade Republic for students starting small.

Bibin's note: "Scalable Capital and Trade Republic are both solid, low-fee options — the choice usually comes down to which app's interface you prefer."
Open Scalable Capital →

Student Discounts
Save money as an international student in Germany
Student Beans / ISIC
Free to access

International student discount platforms — access hundreds of discounts on software, travel, clothing, and tech. ISIC (International Student Identity Card) is accepted worldwide.

Bibin's note: "Get your ISIC card in India before you fly. It works for discounts in Germany and across Europe — worth the small fee."
Get ISIC card →
UNiDAYS
Free membership

A free digital student discount platform covering 300+ brands — from fashion and tech to food delivery — verified through your university email or student ID. Works alongside ISIC rather than replacing it, since the two cover different types of discounts.

Bibin's note: "Sign up for both ISIC and UNiDAYS — they overlap less than you'd expect, and between them you'll catch most of the student deals worth having."

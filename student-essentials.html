01 · Before the visa

Blocked
Account.

Required for your student visa: proof of financial means (€11,904+ for 2026). You're free to choose any provider on the Foreign Office's list.

SperrkontoRecommended

Expatrio

One of the most popular blocked account providers for Indian students. Fast setup, clear process, and it also offers health insurance bundles.

Bibin's note"Setup is straightforward and their support is responsive: useful when you're doing this the first time."

SperrkontoRecommended

Fintiba

The other leading blocked account provider. Strong reputation, clean interface, and quick processing. Many students use Fintiba as their first choice for its simplicity.

Bibin's note"Both Expatrio and Fintiba are solid. Pick the one whose UX feels clearer to you: they're comparable in reliability."

02 · Before you arrive

Health
Insurance.

Mandatory in Germany: you need coverage before you arrive. Public insurers charge students the same statutory base rate; only the additional contribution differs between insurers.

KrankenkassePopular choice

TK (Techniker Krankenkasse)

One of Germany's largest public health insurers. English support, a strong digital experience, and a popular choice among international students for its easy enrolment.

Additional contribution 2026
2.69%
Bibin's note"TK is what most Indian students in my community go with. English support makes a real difference when you first arrive."
Visit TK →
KrankenkasseSolid option

AOK

One of Germany's biggest health insurers, with over 27 million policyholders and more than 100 years of history. It runs English information for international students and a multilingual service hotline. It's organised regionally, so rates differ by AOK.

Additional contribution 2026
2.47–3.5%
Bibin's note"Good English support makes AOK worth a look. It's organised regionally, so just confirm your specific AOK (e.g. AOK Bayern, AOK Nordost) covers your city, and compare its rate, before signing up."
Visit AOK →

03 · Before you fly

Forex & International
Cards.

Move money from India and spend in euros without losing 2–5% (plus GST) to the markup regular Indian cards charge abroad.

Forex debit cardZero forex markup

Niyo Global

An India-based forex debit card built for travellers and students. Load it in INR from any Indian bank account (UPI or IMPS) and spend in euros at the live Visa or Mastercard rate, with no markup fee. It sits on a zero-balance, interest-earning savings account with a partner bank in India.

Bibin's note"This is what most students in the community use to avoid the markup that regular Indian debit cards charge abroad. Order it before you fly: it takes a few days to arrive."
Get Niyo Global →
Multi-currencyMid-market rate

Wise

A multi-currency account and card that lets you hold and spend 40 currencies at the real mid-market exchange rate, including euros once you're in Germany. Useful for receiving money from home or paying in other currencies while travelling.

Bibin's note"Good as a second card for travel around Europe, but you'll still want a proper German bank account like N26 for rent and local direct debits."
Open Wise →
04 · First weeks

SIM Cards &
Mobile Plans.

Stay connected from day one, before and after you arrive.

Network operatorMajor network

Vodafone

One of Germany's major network operators, with strong nationwide coverage and 5G in most cities. Prepaid CallYa plans work well for students who want reliable, contract-free connectivity from day one.

Bibin's note"Vodafone costs a bit more than the discount brands, but the coverage is noticeably better if you're outside a major city."
Visit Vodafone →
SIM-onlyMost data per euro

sim.de

A low-cost SIM-only brand of Drillisch Online (United Internet), running on the 1&1 network. Allnet Flat plans include calls and SMS within Germany, EU roaming and free number transfer. Monthly-cancellable plans are available (these can carry a one-off setup fee). The eSIM is free and activates in minutes; a physical SIM is a one-off €2.99.

Bibin's note"You get far more data per euro here than from the supermarket prepaid SIMs, but this is a contract, not prepaid, so you'll need a German bank account for the direct debit. Start with a prepaid SIM in your first weeks, then move to sim.de once your account is open."
See sim.de plans →
Network operatorGood value

O2

O2 (Telefónica) runs one of Germany's major networks, strong in cities, with competitive prepaid data plans. A solid direct-from-carrier option if you want more data per euro than Vodafone or Telekom.

Bibin's note"O2's own coverage is weaker in rural areas, but if you're studying in a city, it's usually the cheapest of the major networks."
Visit O2 →
PrepaidBudget option

ALDI TALK

A prepaid SIM sold in ALDI supermarkets, running on the O2 network. One of the most popular budget options among students: affordable 4-week data plans with no contract or German bank account required.

Bibin's note"This is what a lot of students in the community use for their first German number: easy to find in any ALDI, and simple to top up."
Visit ALDI TALK →
05 · First week

Banking
in Germany.

Open a German bank account: required for rent, salary and everyday life.

Online bankEasy to open

N26

German online bank with a full English app. One of the easiest accounts to open as an international student: no Anmeldung certificate needed, just a valid ID and a German address. The standard account is free.

Bibin's note"N26 is what I'd recommend opening first: you'll need a German account for rent and almost everything else. One thing to know: you need a German address to apply, so for most students it's a first-week task, not something to set up from India."
Open N26 account →
Branch bankTraditional bank

Sparkasse / Deutsche Bank

If you need a traditional German bank with physical branches: useful for some landlords and employers who prefer established banks for direct debit mandates.

Bibin's note"Some landlords specifically ask for a Sparkasse account. Good to have alongside an N26 as a backup."
Visit Sparkasse →

06 · Once you're settled

Investments
& Savings.

Start building while you study: even small amounts compound over time. Investing carries risk, and the value of ETFs can go down as well as up.

Bank & brokerPopular in Germany

Trade Republic

A Berlin-based, BaFin-supervised bank and investment app: ETFs, stocks and savings plans with very low fees. ETF savings plans are free and start at €1; single trades cost €1. Widely used by Indian students in Germany who want to start investing with small amounts.

Bibin's note"Even €25/month into a broad ETF savings plan adds up fast over a 2-year Master's. Trade Republic makes this very easy."
Open Trade Republic →
Bank & brokerLow-cost broker

Scalable Capital

A Munich-based, BaFin-regulated investment app with its own full banking licence, offering ETF savings plans from €1 with no custody fees on the free plan. A strong low-cost alternative to Trade Republic for students starting small.

Bibin's note"Scalable Capital and Trade Republic are both solid, low-fee options: the choice usually comes down to which app's interface you prefer."
Open Scalable Capital →
07 · Before you fly & after

Student
Discounts.

Save money as an international student in Germany.

Student IDGet it in India

Student Beans / ISIC

International student discount platforms with hundreds of discounts on software, travel, clothing and tech. ISIC (International Student Identity Card) is issued in over 100 countries, including India.

Bibin's note"Get your ISIC card in India before you fly. It works for discounts in Germany and across Europe: worth the small fee."
Get ISIC card →
Discount appFree membership

UNiDAYS

A free student discount platform with offers from hundreds of brands, from fashion and tech to food delivery, verified through your university email. It works alongside ISIC rather than replacing it, since the two cover different types of discounts.

Bibin's note"Sign up for both ISIC and UNiDAYS: they overlap less than you'd expect, and between them you'll catch most of the student deals worth having."
Join UNiDAYS →

Pre-Departure Checklist for Germany

Your admission is confirmed and your visa is sorted or in progress. Here's everything to lock down — academically, financially, medically, and practically — before you board your flight.

Before you start

📌 Your exact list depends on your situation

What you actually need to sort depends on your nationality, visa status, university, degree programme, health-insurance situation, and personal circumstances. Treat your university's instructions and the German authorities responsible for your case as the final authority — this page is a practical planning tool, not a substitute for either.

This guide focuses specifically on what to complete before you leave — it deliberately doesn't repeat the full visa process, the full application process, or the tasks that normally happen after you land. Those are covered in their own dedicated guides, linked as they come up below.

Step 1

Confirm your university is actually ready for you

Before anything else, make sure you're working from your university's official confirmation — not assumptions carried over from someone else's experience.

  • Saved your official admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid) and checked the exact programme, semester, and start date
  • Noted your enrolment (Immatrikulation) deadline
  • Checked whether any online pre-enrolment is required before you travel
  • Checked whether the semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) must be paid before enrolment
  • Saved the International Office's contact details and any arrival instructions they've published
Not every university works the same way. Some expect substantial preparation online before you arrive; others complete key parts of enrolment only after you reach Germany. Your admission letter and student portal take priority over generic advice — including this page.

Still assembling academic documents or unsure about your application status? The University Application Checklist → covers that stage in full.

Step 2

Visa & immigration — confirm, don't assume

Whether you need an entry visa at all depends on your nationality. EU/EEA and Swiss citizens generally don't need one. Many other applicants do need a national visa before travelling, while some can enter and apply for the residence permit afterward — always check your individual case against the Federal Foreign Office's current guidance and the mission responsible for you.

✈️ Don't book around a guessed visa date

Student visa processing can take several weeks and, in individual cases, considerably longer. Avoid large non-refundable travel commitments until your visa is actually in hand, unless you're comfortable with the financial risk if the date needs to shift.

  • Visa received, and passport checked immediately
  • Visa validity period, number of entries, and any conditions/annotations verified as correct
  • Copies of the visa saved, both physically and digitally

This page assumes your visa is sorted or close to it. For the full application walkthrough, see the Student Visa Checklist →.

Step 3

Money: proof of funds, everyday cash & customs

If your case uses a blocked account (Sperrkonto)

Current benchmark
€11,904 / year
Equivalent to €992/month. Set by the Federal Foreign Office and reviewed periodically — confirm the live figure before transferring funds. Federal Foreign Office ↗
Not the only method
Check your case
Financial proof can also come from a qualifying Declaration of Commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) or an eligible scholarship, depending on your situation.

If your visa case does use a blocked account, make sure the required amount is deposited and the confirmation letter is saved — both digitally and printed — well before your appointment or travel.

Money for your first days

Don't plan around your blocked-account payout landing in your pocket the moment you arrive. Prepare a working combination instead:

International debit/credit card Card works internationally Foreign transaction fees checked PIN known Some euro cash Emergency funds kept separate Banking app accessible
💶 Carrying €10,000 or more in cash?

German and EU customs rules require a declaration for cash or equivalent means of payment worth €10,000 or more when entering or leaving the country — "it's my own money" doesn't remove the requirement. The exact procedure depends on whether you're arriving from an EU or a non-EU country. German Customs — cash declaration ↗

Step 4

Health insurance

Germany requires health-insurance coverage, and which system applies to you — statutory (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung) or a private/exempt route — depends on your age, programme, and circumstances.

  • Confirmed which system applies to you, and contacted the relevant insurer
  • Completed any required registration, and saved your own confirmation
  • Confirmed how your status will reach your university
  • Considered travel health insurance for the gap before German coverage starts
Many statutory insurers can transmit the required confirmation electronically, directly to your university — worth confirming with your chosen insurer so nothing gets lost between the two.
⚠️ Travel insurance ≠ German student health insurance

They serve different purposes. Buying travel insurance for your flight doesn't automatically satisfy your university's health-insurance requirement.

Step 5

Accommodation

Before departure, you should have a realistic plan for where you'll actually sleep — permanent or temporary.

🔑 If it's secured
  • Signed rental agreement (Mietvertrag) saved
  • Move-in date & key handover confirmed
  • Deposit (Kaution) amount & payment method confirmed
  • What's included in rent checked
🕐 If it's not yet
  • Temporary accommodation booked, with check-in confirmed
  • Access outside normal reception hours checked
  • International Office / Studierendenwerk contact saved
  • Time budgeted to search after arrival
🚩 Accommodation scams are real

Never assume a listing is legitimate just because someone sent a contract or ID. Be especially cautious of pressure to transfer money immediately, before you've verified the property, landlord, and contract.

Step 6

Travel arrangements

Book your journey once your situation is genuinely certain — ideally with visa in hand.

Passport validity checked Baggage allowance Cabin dimensions Battery & liquids rules Booking saved offline Route to accommodation saved Airport pickup arranged, if needed
🛂 First entry into Germany? EES doesn't apply to you

The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully live since 10 April 2026 — but it does not apply to you. EES records non-EU travellers arriving for short stays. Holders of a long-stay national visa (Type D) or a residence permit are not registered in it, and no fingerprints or facial scan are taken: you show your passport together with your visa and the check runs as it always has. Arrival halls may still move slowly while short-stay travellers are processed, so don't book a tight onward connection. European Commission — who is exempt ↗

Connecting through another country? Check that country's entry/transit rules too — not just Germany's.

Step 7

Your document folder — physical & digital

This is one of the most important parts of pre-departure prep: one physical folder for your hand luggage, one secure digital backup.

🛂Identity & immigration
Passport, visa, passport photos if relevant, and copies of both the passport ID page and the visa.
🎓University & academic
Admission letter, enrolment/pre-enrolment confirmation, transcripts and certificates, and — where they apply — your APS certificate, uni-assist documents, VPD, or language certificate.
💶Financial & insurance
Financial-proof documentation (blocked-account confirmation or equivalent), bank details, and your health-insurance confirmation.
🏠Accommodation
Rental contract or temporary-accommodation confirmation, plus the landlord's or provider's contact details.
Digital backup: keep copies in more than one place — phone, cloud, and a physical set — and make sure the essentials are accessible even without internet. A simple folder structure works well:
Passport & Visa University Academic Documents Financial Insurance Accommodation Travel Emergency Contacts
Step 8

Phone, SIM & internet

Make sure you can communicate the moment you land.

  • Home SIM kept active long enough to receive OTPs
  • Roaming or a temporary eSIM arranged
  • Phone unlocked, if you plan to use a German SIM
  • Offline maps downloaded, important numbers saved
  • Two-factor authentication checked against a possible SIM change
📵 A SIM here isn't instantly active

German law requires identity verification before any SIM — prepaid or postpaid — is activated. Buying one at the airport doesn't mean it works immediately; budget for a short verification step in your first day or two.

Step 9

Medication & medical preparation

If you take regular medication, prepare this properly before you fly.

  • Medicines in their original packaging, with the prescription or medical documentation
  • Active ingredient of each medicine known, in case it's asked about
  • Carried in hand luggage where appropriate
German Customs treats normal personal need as up to roughly a three-month supply of the recommended dose, per medicine. German Customs — medicinal products ↗
💊 Controlled or narcotic medication is stricter

For journeys of up to 30 days within the Schengen area, you'll need a certificate issued under Article 75 of the Schengen Agreement — completed by your prescribing doctor and authenticated before you travel. Don't rely on a generic doctor's letter for a controlled medicine; check the exact requirement early. BfArM — travelling with controlled drugs ↗

Step 10

Packing your luggage

🎒 Hand luggage
  • Documents (see above)
  • Phone, laptop, chargers, adapters
  • Essential medication
  • One change of clothes
  • Wallet, cards, limited cash
🧳 Checked luggage
  • Weather-appropriate clothing & layers
  • Comfortable shoes, rain protection
  • Study & personal items you actually need
  • Remaining toiletries
Airline limits checked Prohibited items checked Battery rules checked Customs restrictions checked Luggage weighed & labelled
🎒 Never check these

Your passport, visa, admission letter, and essential financial, insurance, and medical documents belong in your hand luggage — not checked baggage.

The last stretch

Your final countdown

Use the last two weeks to verify, not to solve major problems for the first time. If something's genuinely broken this late, that's a signal to reach out to your university — not to keep improvising alone.

2 weeks out: verify docs, money, insurance, travel & university status
→
48 hours out: passport in hand luggage, devices charged, luggage within limits
→
Day of departure: family has your itinerary, transfer confirmed

Before you leave, share your flight details, arrival address, and emergency contacts with someone you trust at home — so they can actually help if something goes wrong while you're travelling.

You don't have to finish everything before you fly. Residential registration (Anmeldung), opening a local current account (Girokonto), final university enrolment, and — where required — applying for your residence permit are normally sorted after arrival, guided by your university and the local authorities. Don't force post-arrival tasks into this list. For exactly what to prioritize once you land, see the First Week in Germany Checklist →
Learn from others

Common mistakes

  • ✕Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is secured. Avoid large commitments until your immigration status is confirmed.
  • ✕Keeping documents only digitally. Some situations genuinely need originals — carry them, plus copies.
  • ✕Packing your passport or visa in checked baggage. Keep essentials with you at all times.
  • ✕Assuming every university works the same way. Deadlines and processes genuinely differ.
  • ✕Assuming a blocked account is the only valid financial proof. Other recognised methods exist depending on your case.
  • ✕Assuming travel insurance covers your student health-insurance requirement. They're not the same thing.
  • ✕Carrying medication without checking the rules. Especially risky for controlled substances.
  • ✕Trusting an accommodation listing without verifying it. Don't transfer money just because someone sent a contract.
  • ✕Leaving your SIM/authentication setup to the last day. A changed number can lock you out of banking and accounts right when you need them.
From experience

Bibin's Advice

👨‍🎓
Bibin Johnson

The week before I flew out, I wasn't worried about the big things — my visa and admission were already sorted. What actually cost me time was the small stuff: figuring out how much cash actually made sense to carry, and realising my Indian SIM would stop receiving OTPs sooner than I expected. I'd fix that a few days earlier if I were doing it again.

The one habit that genuinely helped: I scanned every document I was carrying and put it in a labelled folder in the cloud before I left, not after. The first week in a new country is not when you want to be searching your inbox for an admission letter.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a blocked account before travelling?+
Not necessarily. It's one recognised way to prove financial means — a Declaration of Commitment or scholarship may also apply depending on your case.
How much money is required in a blocked account?+
The current benchmark is €11,904/year (€992/month) — always verify the live figure with the Federal Foreign Office before transferring funds.
Do I need health insurance before I fly?+
You need appropriate coverage for Germany, and it's normally part of your visa/enrolment process — travel insurance for the flight isn't a substitute.
Documents in hand luggage or checked luggage?+
Passport, visa, admission letter, and essential financial/insurance documents always stay in hand luggage.
Should I book my flight before my visa arrives?+
Avoid non-refundable bookings until your visa is issued, unless you're comfortable with the risk of changing the date.
Can I bring my regular medicines?+
Generally yes, in personal-use quantities — German Customs treats up to roughly a three-month supply per medicine as normal.
What if my medication is controlled?+
You'll likely need an Article 75 Schengen certificate from your doctor, authenticated before travel — check the exact rule for your medicine with BfArM.
Do I need to declare cash when entering Germany?+
Yes, if you're carrying €10,000 or more in cash or equivalent — the exact procedure depends on where you're arriving from.
Do I need a German SIM before I travel?+
No, but make sure you can get online and receive OTPs after landing — and note that any German SIM needs identity verification before it activates.
My university hasn't sent arrival info yet — what now?+
Contact the International Office directly rather than relying on generic advice — arrival procedures genuinely differ between universities.

Before you start

📌 Your exact list depends on your situation

What you actually need to sort depends on your nationality, visa status, university, degree programme, health-insurance situation and personal circumstances. Treat your university's instructions and the German authorities responsible for your case as the final authority: this page is a practical planning tool, not a substitute for either.

This guide focuses specifically on what to complete before you leave. It deliberately doesn't repeat the full visa process, the full application process, or the tasks that normally happen after you land; those are covered in their own guides, linked as they come up below.

Work through it roughly in this order, since some tasks only make sense once an earlier one is done:

  1. University
  2. Visa
  3. Money
  4. Health insurance
  5. Accommodation
  6. Travel
  7. Documents
  8. Phone & packing
  9. Final countdown
🎓Gate02
Step 1

Confirm your university is actually ready for you

Before anything else, make sure you're working from your university's official confirmation, not assumptions carried over from someone else's experience.

  • Saved your official admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid) and checked the exact programme, semester and start date
  • Noted your enrolment (Immatrikulation) deadline
  • Checked whether any online pre-enrolment is required before you travel
  • Checked whether the semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) must be paid before enrolment
  • Saved the International Office's contact details and any arrival instructions they've published

Not every university works the same way. Some expect substantial preparation online before you arrive; others complete key parts of enrolment only after you reach Germany. Your admission letter and student portal take priority over generic advice, including this page.

Still assembling academic documents or unsure about your application status? The University Application Checklist → covers that stage in full.

🛂Gate03
Step 2

Visa & immigration: confirm, don't assume

Whether you need an entry visa at all depends on your nationality. EU/EEA and Swiss citizens generally don't need one. Many other applicants, including Indian students, need a national visa before travelling, while some nationalities can enter and apply for the residence permit afterwards: always check your individual case against the Federal Foreign Office's current guidance and the mission responsible for you.

HOLD
✈️ Don't book around a guessed visa date

The Federal Foreign Office says applications for longer stays may take several months to process, depending on the purpose of the stay. Avoid large non-refundable travel commitments until your visa is actually in hand, unless you're comfortable with the financial risk if the date needs to shift.

  • Visa received, and passport checked immediately
  • Visa validity period, number of entries, and any conditions/annotations verified as correct
  • Copies of the visa saved, both physically and digitally

This page assumes your visa is sorted or close to it. For the full application walkthrough, see the Student Visa Checklist →.

Step 3

Money: proof of funds, everyday cash & customs

If your case uses a blocked account (Sperrkonto)

Current benchmark€11,904 / year

Equivalent to €992/month. Reviewed periodically: confirm the live figure before transferring funds. Make it in Germany ↗ Federal Foreign Office ↗

Not the only methodCheck your case

Financial proof can also come from a qualifying Declaration of Commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) or an eligible scholarship, depending on your situation.

If your visa case does use a blocked account, make sure the required amount is deposited and the confirmation letter is saved, both digitally and printed, well before your appointment or travel.

Money for your first days

Don't plan around your blocked-account payout landing in your pocket the moment you arrive. Prepare a working combination instead:

💳 International debit/credit card🌍 Card works internationally🧾 Foreign transaction fees checked🔢 PIN known💶 Some euro cash🛟 Emergency funds kept separate📱 Banking app accessible
● Goods to declare💶 Carrying €10,000 or more in cash?

German and EU customs rules require a declaration for cash or equivalent means of payment worth €10,000 or more when entering or leaving the country; "it's my own money" doesn't remove the requirement. The exact procedure depends on whether you're arriving from an EU or a non-EU country.

Arriving from a non-EU country such as India, you must declare it in writing, immediately and without being asked. German Customs offers the form in English (form 040001), and air travellers hand it in at the red exit. German Customs: cash declaration ↗

✚Gate05
Step 4

Health insurance

Germany requires health-insurance coverage, and which system applies to you, statutory (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung) or a private/exempt route, depends on your age, programme and circumstances.

  • Confirmed which system applies to you, and contacted the relevant insurer
  • Completed any required registration, and saved your own confirmation
  • Confirmed how your status will reach your university
  • Considered travel health insurance for the gap before German coverage starts

Many statutory insurers can transmit the required confirmation electronically, directly to your university: worth confirming with your chosen insurer so nothing gets lost between the two. See recommended insurers →

⌂Gate06
Step 5

Accommodation

Before departure, you should have a realistic plan for where you'll actually sleep, permanent or temporary.

🔑 If it's secured
  • Signed rental agreement (Mietvertrag) saved
  • Move-in date & key handover confirmed
  • Deposit (Kaution) amount & payment method confirmed
  • What's included in rent checked
🕐 If it's not yet
  • Temporary accommodation booked, with check-in confirmed
  • Access outside normal reception hours checked
  • International Office / Studierendenwerk contact saved
  • Time budgeted to search after arrival
🚩 Accommodation scams are real

Never assume a listing is legitimate just because someone sent a contract or ID. Be especially cautious of pressure to transfer money immediately, before you've verified the property, landlord and contract.

✈Gate07
Step 6

Travel arrangements

Book your journey once your situation is certain, ideally with visa in hand.

Passport validity checkedBaggage allowanceCabin dimensionsBattery & liquids rulesBooking saved offlineRoute to accommodation savedAirport pickup arranged, if needed
🛂 First entry into Germany? EES doesn't apply to you

The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational since 10 April 2026, but it does not apply to you. EES records non-EU travellers arriving for short stays. Holders of a German long-stay national visa (Type D) or residence permit are not registered in it, and no fingerprints or facial scan are taken: you show your passport together with your visa, as before. Arrival halls may still move slowly while short-stay travellers are processed, so don't book a tight onward connection. European Commission: who is exempt ↗

Connecting through another country? Check that country's entry/transit rules too, not just Germany's.

▤Gate08
Step 7

Your document folder: physical & digital

This is one of the most important parts of pre-departure prep: one physical folder for your hand luggage, one secure digital backup.

🛂 Identity & immigration

Passport, visa, passport photos if relevant, and copies of both the passport ID page and the visa.

🎓 University & academic

Admission letter, enrolment/pre-enrolment confirmation, transcripts and certificates, and, where they apply, your APS certificate, uni-assist documents, VPD or language certificate.

💶 Financial & insurance

Financial-proof documentation (blocked-account confirmation or equivalent), bank details, and your health-insurance confirmation.

🏠 Accommodation

Rental contract or temporary-accommodation confirmation, plus the landlord's or provider's contact details.

Digital backup: keep copies in more than one place (phone, cloud, and a physical set) and make sure the essentials are accessible even without internet. A simple folder structure works well:

Phone, SIM & internet

Make sure you can communicate the moment you land.

No serviceNew German SIMID check needed before it works

  • Roaming or a temporary eSIM arranged
  • Phone unlocked, if you plan to use a German SIM

📵 A SIM here isn't instantly active

German law requires identity verification before any SIM, prepaid or postpaid, is activated. Buying one at the airport doesn't mean it works immediately; budget for a short verification step in your first day or two. SIM options →

💊Gate10
Step 9

Medication & medical preparation

If you take regular medication, prepare this properly before you fly.

  • Medicines in their original packaging, with the prescription or medical documentation
  • Active ingredient of each medicine known, in case it's asked about
  • Carried in hand luggage where appropriate
Month 1Month 2Month 3

German Customs defines personal need as a maximum of three months' supply of the recommended dose of each medicinal product. German Customs: medicinal products ↗

💊 Controlled or narcotic medication is stricter

Flying in from India, you're arriving from outside the Schengen area, so the Schengen "Article 75" certificate doesn't apply to you. Germany's Federal Opium Agency (BfArM) recommends the International Narcotics Control Board's guidelines instead: carry a doctor's certificate stating your individual and daily dosage, the international name of the active substance, and the duration of your journey. There are no harmonised international rules, so also check the national rules of Germany and any transit country for your specific medicine, early. (The Article 75 certificate, valid up to 30 days, is for trips from other Schengen countries, for example once you live in Germany.) BfArM: travelling with controlled drugs ↗

⏱Gate12
The last stretch

Your final countdown

Use the last two weeks to verify, not to solve major problems for the first time. If something's broken this late, that's a signal to reach out to your university, not to keep improvising alone.

  1. T − 14 days

    Verify docs, money, insurance, travel & university status

  2. T − 48 hours

    Passport in hand luggage, devices charged, luggage within limits

  3. Departure day

    Family has your itinerary, transfer confirmed

Set your departure date on the pass at the top of the page and these turn into real dates.

Before you leave, share your flight details, arrival address and emergency contacts with someone you trust at home, so they can actually help if something goes wrong while you're travelling.

You don't have to finish everything before you fly

Residential registration (Anmeldung), opening a local current account (Girokonto), final university enrolment and, where required, applying for your residence permit are normally sorted after arrival, guided by your university and the local authorities. Don't force post-arrival tasks into this list. For exactly what to prioritise once you land, see the First Week in Germany Checklist →

✕Gate13
Learn from others

Common mistakes

  • Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is secured. Avoid large commitments until your immigration status is confirmed.
  • Keeping documents only digitally. Some situations need originals: carry them, plus copies.
  • Packing your passport or visa in checked baggage. Keep essentials with you at all times.
  • Assuming every university works the same way. Deadlines and processes differ.
  • Assuming a blocked account is the only valid financial proof. Other recognised methods exist depending on your case.
  • Assuming travel insurance covers your student health-insurance requirement. They're not the same thing.
  • Carrying medication without checking the rules. Especially risky for controlled substances.
  • Trusting an accommodation listing without verifying it. Don't transfer money just because someone sent a contract.
  • Leaving your SIM/authentication setup to the last day. A changed number can lock you out of banking and accounts right when you need them.
14 · 💡 From experience

Bibin's advice

The week before I flew out, I wasn't worried about the big things: my visa and admission were already sorted. What actually cost me time was the small stuff: figuring out how much cash actually made sense to carry, and realising my Indian SIM would stop receiving OTPs sooner than I expected. I'd fix that a few days earlier if I were doing it again.

The one habit that helped: I scanned every document I was carrying and put it in a labelled folder in the cloud before I left, not after. The first week in a new country is not when you want to be searching your inbox for an admission letter.

Nr.01
The basics

What this visa is, and why it matters

The German Student Visa, officially the National Visa (Type D), is what allows non-EU students to enter Germany for long-term study. It's not the final step: once you land, you'll convert it into a residence permit at your local Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde).

  1. In IndiaNational Visa (Type D)
  2. You landGermany
  3. AusländerbehördeResidence permit

Most visa delays don't come from the mission being slow: they come from applicants forgetting a document, carrying the wrong photocopies, having insurance that hasn't started yet, or showing financial proof that doesn't quite meet the current amount. A complete, correctly ordered application is the single biggest thing in your control.

Nr.02
📌 What's changed for 2026

Two updates worth knowing

Applies to youConsular Services Portal (CSP)

The CSP is now the online starting point for most national visa applications: the German missions in India say the majority of national visas can be applied for online there, including "study purposes and seeking a university place". You submit your application digitally and get feedback on whether your documents are complete, so the appointment itself is mostly originals, biometrics and the fee.

For visa types not available online, you apply on paper: book the appointment through the service provider VFS at the location responsible for your place of residence. Follow whatever the portal or your mission's page shows for your case. Federal Foreign Office: CSP ↗ German missions in India: national visa ↗

Doesn't apply to youEES (Entry/Exit System)

The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational since 10 April 2026. It records non-EU travellers on short stays. Holders of a long-stay national visa (Type D) or a residence permit issued by Germany are not registered in it, so no fingerprints or facial scan are taken at the border: you show your passport together with your visa, as before.

Arrival queues may still be longer than usual while short-stay travellers are processed, so don't plan a tight onward connection. European Commission: who is exempt ↗

Nr.04
The full list

Document checklist

Your exact document list depends on your visa category, nationality, and the German mission responsible for your application: always confirm the current requirements on your mission's page before your appointment. The documents below follow the German missions in India's student visa checklist ↗. Go through this slowly: a single missing document or expired insurance policy is enough to delay your entire appointment.

  1. 01Passport

    Issued within the last 10 years, with at least two empty pages and in good condition. Passports with remarks on the data page aren't accepted. Add an A4 copy of the data page.

  2. 02Visa Application Form

    Completed via the CSP portal (see the 2026 update above). Every detail must match your passport exactly, with no spelling variations, and it must be personally signed. The checklist also asks for the signed declaration under Section 54 of the Residence Act and a declaration with additional contact and legal-representation details.

  3. 03Passport Photographs

    Three biometric photos, not older than six months (plain background, correct dimensions). Check the exact current spec on your visa application centre or mission's page before printing: this can change.

  4. 04University Admission Letter

    Confirm it clearly shows the university name, course name, semester and date before you rely on it.

  5. 05If applicableAPS Certificate

    Bring it along with copies. It's issued as a PDF, so print it and keep the original file unchanged. Full APS guide here →

  6. 06Academic Documents

    The original and two copies of every qualification you have: Class X, Class XII, Bachelor's degree and transcripts, and Master's documents if applicable.

  7. 07CriticalProof of Financial Resources

    One of the most important documents in your entire application, covered in full in the next section, since there are several valid routes and this is where most avoidable rejections happen.

  8. 08Health Insurance

    Must cover Germany and be valid from your intended travel date, not from some later date you'll "sort out on arrival". The German missions in India check that it covers the visa's starting date: if your cover starts later than your intended travel date, the visa's start date is adjusted to match. Their checklist asks for travel health insurance from your arrival until your enrolment, when student health insurance takes over. See recommended providers →

  9. 09Motivation Letter

    The checklist asks for a statement of purpose or motivation letter. Cover why Germany, why this university, your career goals and your future plans. Keep it honest and concise: this isn't the place to overreach.

  10. 10Curriculum Vitae (CV)

    Clean, chronological format covering education, work experience, internships, skills and languages.

  11. 11If applicableLanguage Certificate

    Needed unless your admission letter already confirms your language skills: proof in the language your course is taught in, as a rule at least B2 if there's no preparatory language course. Only recognised certificates count, such as Goethe-Institut, ÖSD, IELTS, TOEFL or Cambridge. Exemption letters, "language of instruction" certificates and certificates from online exams aren't accepted.

  12. 12Visa Fee

    €75 for a national visa, paid in rupees: ₹8,400 (₹4,200 for minors). The accepted payment methods depend on your mission or application centre. Details in the Fee & Timeline section below.

  13. 13Appointment Confirmation

    Printed, not just on your phone.

  14. 14Photocopies

    Two identical sets of everything above, with your originals alongside, as the checklist asks. Don't staple anything, and never assume the visa centre will make copies for you.

Organise before you go

Arrange documents in this order. Labelled dividers or a transparent folder make your appointment noticeably smoother:

  1. Passport
  2. Application Form
  3. Photos
  4. Admission Letter
  5. APS Certificate
  6. Financial Proof

Nr.05
The critical one

Proof of financial resources

This is the document Bibin's own notes flag as one of the most important in the whole application, and it's where avoidable rejections happen most. You have a few valid routes: pick the one that actually fits your situation.

Most common · Blocked Account€11,904 for the year

With a monthly withdrawal cap of €992. This is the standard route for most students without a sponsor. The amount changes periodically: always check the current figure before opening your account, not the number you saw a few months ago. Make it in Germany: studying ↗ Federal Foreign Office: blocked account ↗ See recommended blocked account providers →

JanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSepOctNovDec12 × €992 = €11,904
Declaration of commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung)

A German resident formally guarantees your costs at their local Foreigners' Office. A real alternative if you have someone in Germany able to sponsor you, but it depends entirely on having that person, so it isn't an option for most applicants.

Scholarship Confirmation

DAAD or another recognised scholarship covering your costs can substitute for a blocked account, if your confirmation letter clearly states the amount and duration.

Other Officially Accepted Proof

Less common, case-specific proof is sometimes accepted: confirm directly with your mission rather than assuming.

If your university charges tuition

For example TUM, or the public universities in Baden-Württemberg for non-EU students. The German missions' checklist then also asks for proof of funds for the first two semesters' fees and the fee structure: a payment confirmation from the university, an education loan, or an extra deposit on top of the €11,904 in your blocked account. Cost & time

Fee & processing time

Visa fee€75
In rupees₹8,400
Minors€37.50 · ₹4,200
VFS service fee€10–20, paid in INR

The German missions in India list the national visa fee as ₹8,400 (₹4,200 for minors), paid in rupees only. VFS's own fee page still showed ₹7,600 when checked on 1 Oct 2026, and it points to the mission's page for the current rupee amount, so go by the mission.

The payment method depends on your mission or application centre (the German missions in India list demand draft or cash, depending on the location): check their current instructions rather than assuming. The external service provider charges every applicant a separate service fee, approved by the German government, of between €10 and €20, paid in INR at the current exchange rate. German missions in India: national visa fee ↗ Payment methods ↗

Processing timeUp to several months

The Federal Foreign Office says applications for longer stays "may take several months to process, depending on the purpose of the stay", and the German missions in India say the same for national visas. Appointments can also be months away: early appointments are only given in extreme medical or humanitarian situations. So apply as early as reasonably possible. Nr.07
Step by step

How to actually apply

The document checklist tells you what to gather. This is how it actually gets submitted:

  1. Confirm your document set is complete

    Admission letter, APS (if applicable), financial proof and insurance in place before you start the clock.

  2. Register and apply via the CSP portal

    The online starting point for most national visas, including study: check the portal for whether it's available for your specific case and mission.

  3. Pay the visa fee

    €75, paid as ₹8,400 (minors €37.50, ₹4,200). Keep the receipt as part of your document set.

  4. Book your appointment

    Start this early: appointments can be months away. For paper applications in India, you book through VFS at the location responsible for your place of residence. Confirm which centre is responsible for you before booking. VFS Global's Germany booking guide ↗

  5. Attend your appointment

    Submit your organised documents, biometrics are taken, and there's a short verification conversation: more on this below.

  6. Track your application

    Use the portal to check status rather than calling repeatedly.

  7. Collect your visa

    Your passport with the visa affixed, once issued.

  8. Only then book your flights

    Not before. Covered in Common Mistakes below, because it's the one that costs people real money.

Nr.08
Avoid these

Common mistakes

  • Booking flights before visa approval. Do not book non-refundable flights until your visa is actually issued: this is the mistake that costs people real money.
  • Incorrect blocked account amount. Confirm the current required amount at the time you apply, not whatever figure you saw months ago.
  • Insurance starting too late. Your coverage needs to begin from your intended travel date, not "whenever it's convenient".
  • Name spelling that doesn't match across documents. Your name should be identical across your passport, APS, admission letter, insurance, blocked account and visa form.
  • Poor document organisation. Messy files create unnecessary stress during an appointment that's stressful enough already.
  • Missing photocopies. Carry multiple copies of every important document: don't assume the centre will make them for you.
09 · 💡 Bibin's advice

Three folders, not one

If I were applying today, I'd prepare three separate folders:

📁Folder 1Originals
📁Folder 2Photocopies
☁️Folder 3Digital backupGoogle Drive or OneDrive · phone · USB drive

For the digital backup, don't rely on just one place: put it on Google Drive or OneDrive, your phone, and a USB drive. Losing a document while you're abroad is stressful. Having it backed up in three places is the difference between a small inconvenience and a real problem.

Nr.10
The appointment

Visa interview tips

During your appointment, a few things help:

Answer honestly: don't say what you think they want to hear

Speak clearly and don't rush

Don't memorise answers word-for-word: it reads as rehearsed, not confident

Why this course?

Know why you chose your specific course, not just "why Germany"

How will you finance your studies?

Know exactly how you'll finance your studies: this comes up often

Stay calm if you're asked something unexpected

Remember

This is generally a verification of your application, not a test designed to catch you out. If your documents are complete and honest, the conversation is usually straightforward.

One honest note

Visa requirements, accepted financial proof and blocked account amounts change. Always verify the latest requirements on the official website of the German Embassy or Consulate responsible for your application, or your official visa service provider, before your appointment. This guide is here to help you prepare efficiently, but official instructions take precedence if the two ever differ.

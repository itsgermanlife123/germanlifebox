Before you start

The right choice depends on more than demand

Your Ausbildung choice comes down to what you enjoy doing, what you're good at, how much German you can use professionally, whether you prefer people, technology, business or practical work, your educational background, the kind of workplace you want, and where you want your career to go afterwards.

⚠️ The single most common mistake

Don't choose an Ausbildung just because it's "in demand." Choose one whose actual day-to-day work you can see yourself doing for several years — because that's how long it lasts, and a shortage occupation you dislike is still a bad career choice.

⚠️ Education & social care doesn't follow the dual model

Not everything called "Ausbildung" is the same thing. Erzieher/in has several training models — school-based routes, practice-integrated PiA, and other state-specific structures — which differ substantially in how they're organised and whether you're paid.

Don't compare an Erzieher/in programme against a standard dual Ausbildung just because both use the word. Check the training model, the Bundesland and the payment arrangement before you commit to anything.

🎯 The question that actually decides it

Would you still want this profession if the salary and the "Germany" factor were removed?

If the answer is no, reconsider the occupation. You'll be doing this work every day for two to three and a half years, and then for a career after that.

Read this carefully

What the German level indicators mean

The coloured labels in the finder are German LifeBox planning indicators — our read on how much German the work realistically demands. They are not legal requirements, and no employer is bound by them.

🟢B1 may be workable
More realistic where the work is strongly practical, communication is relatively straightforward, and technical skill carries most of the job — some skilled trades, industrial and technical occupations, warehouse and production roles. This does not mean an employer must accept B1.
🟡B2 often makes sense
Where you need substantial customer communication, documentation, telephone work, business correspondence or technical discussion — kaufmännische occupations, retail, hospitality front of house, healthcare support roles.
🔴Higher or occupation-specific
Where the occupation carries its own requirements, or communication is so central that a lower level would leave you unable to do the job. Nursing is the clearest case: training requires B1 or B2 depending on the federal state, plus proof of physical and mental fitness and a certificate of good conduct. Separately, B2 is generally required in most states for professional recognition of a nursing qualification. Make it in Germany — nursing profile ↗
📌 Visa B1 ≠ employer acceptance

For third-country nationals, current government guidance sets B1 as the normal benchmark for the vocational-training visa — specifically where the training institution hasn't already tested your German and you haven't completed a preparatory course.

That clears immigration. It does not oblige a single employer to hire you, and it doesn't mean you'll cope in Berufsschule. Those are three different bars. Make it in Germany — vocational training visa ↗

A warning

Don't choose an Ausbildung from a salary table

Your training pay varies significantly by occupation, employer, collective agreement, Bundesland, training year, and whether the employer is public or private. Two people in the same occupation in different states can be paid noticeably differently.

Use salary as one factor, not your main selection criterion. When you shortlist an occupation, look at the actual vacancies you're interested in and compare their stated Ausbildungsvergütung — that's a real number for a real job, rather than a national average that describes nobody.

Step 4

Research your final three

Once you've narrowed to three occupations, work through all four steps for each one. This is the part most applicants skip, and it's the part that separates a targeted application from a wasted year.

01Check the official occupation profile
Search the occupation in BERUFENET, the Federal Employment Agency's occupation database. It gives you the detailed picture of tasks, training route and alternatives — including occupations similar to the one you searched, which is often how people find a better fit. Open BERUFENET ↗
02Confirm it's officially recognised
Use the BIBB Berufesuche and the current register. It tells you the training duration, the legal basis, any specialisations, the training structure and the DQR classification. If an occupation isn't in there under that name, the name is wrong or out of date. BIBB Berufesuche ↗
03Run Check-U
Check-U is the Federal Employment Agency's free career-orientation tool. It tests your competencies and interests and matches them against the requirements of actual Ausbildung occupations. It takes around 80 minutes and it's worth the time — it will suggest occupations you hadn't considered. Start Check-U ↗
04Look at 10–20 real vacancies
Search the occupation on the Federal Employment Agency's Jobsuche and on employer career pages. Don't ask "is this profession in demand?" — ask "are companies actually hiring someone with my profile?" Record what each vacancy asks for: school qualification, German level, other languages, previous experience, technical skills, location, pay, start date. Twenty vacancies will tell you more than twenty articles. BA Jobsuche ↗

Common questions

Frequently asked

How many Ausbildung professions are there?+
BIBB's 2026 register lists 324 occupations recognised under BBiG/HwO, or 325 including Schiffsmechaniker/in. The register is compiled annually with a 1 October cut-off, so names and training regulations do change between editions.
Which Ausbildung is best for international students?+
There isn't one. It depends on your education, German, interests, strengths, work preferences, which employers are hiring your profile, and your long-term plans. Anyone offering a single universal answer is selling something.
Which Ausbildung can I do with B1 German?+
B1 is the normal government benchmark for the vocational-training visa, but employers and occupations can expect more. Practical and technical occupations are often more realistic around B1 — but that's a planning observation, not a legal classification.
Is IT better than engineering?+
Neither is inherently better. IT suits people who enjoy software, systems and computer-based problem-solving. Engineering suits people who prefer machines, electronics, manufacturing and hands-on work. Choose on the actual work, not the sector label.
Is healthcare good for international applicants?+
It can be, but healthcare carries occupation-specific requirements and much higher communication demands. Nursing training requires B1 or B2 depending on the federal state, plus proof of fitness and a certificate of good conduct. Research the profession, not the sector.
Can I choose without knowing what I want?+
Yes — that's exactly what orientation tools are for. Start from interests and strengths, build a shortlist of three to five, then investigate each properly. Check-U is designed for this.
Should I choose a shortage occupation?+
Not on its own. A shortage can improve your prospects, but it doesn't make an occupation suitable for you. A shortage occupation you hate is still a bad career choice.
Can I apply for several professions?+
Yes, and applying to several suitable occupations usually improves your chances. But keep them targeted — don't send the same generic application to completely unrelated occupations. Employers notice immediately.
Is every Ausbildung paid?+
No. Standard dual company-based Ausbildung normally pays a training allowance, but school-based programmes can be structured very differently. Erzieher/in is the clearest example — models vary by state and so do the payment arrangements.

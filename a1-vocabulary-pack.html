Start here

Learn words you'll actually use

Learning German gets much easier once you stop memorizing random words and start with vocabulary you'll genuinely use. This pack focuses on high-frequency A1 words for everyday communication, plus the situations international students and newcomers commonly run into in Germany.

The one habit

Learn the article with every noun. Don't learn Wohnung = apartment. Learn die Wohnung = apartment. That small habit pays off enormously once you reach German grammar.

Wohnung = apartmentdie Wohnung = apartment
der masculinedie femininedas neuter

This colour coding runs through every list below: the more you see it, the more it sticks.

Recommended method
  1. Learn 10–15 words at a time
  2. Learn the article with every noun
  3. Say each word aloud
  4. Read the example sentence
  5. Create your own sentence
  6. Review yesterday's words before learning new ones
  7. Practise using the words in speaking, not just recognizing them
  8. Mark difficult words and review them again
📌 This isn't the official Goethe A1 list

This is a practical German LifeBox vocabulary pack, not a reproduction of the official Goethe-Zertifikat A1 vocabulary list. The Goethe-Institut publishes its own official A1 Wortliste for Start Deutsch 1: see Official sources ↓ for the direct link.

Words on their own get you surprisingly far, and then they stop. Everything below is the other half: what to actually say at the counter, on the phone, at the Bürgeramt and in the waiting room. It's the German a classroom tends not to cover, because it is not grammar; it is just what people say.

German for situations a classroom never covers

Moving to Germany means using German in situations you may never have practiced in a classroom. You may know hundreds of German words and still struggle when someone asks you a question at the supermarket, a receptionist answers the phone quickly, a train announcement changes, or you need to explain something at the Bürgeramt.

This guide focuses on complete phrases you can actually use, for:

  • Shopping
  • Restaurants
  • Public transport
  • University
  • Work
  • Housing
  • Appointments
  • Doctors & pharmacies
  • Banks
  • Phone calls
  • Behörden
  • Asking for clarification
  • Everyday conversation
  • Handling problems
Tip: Don't try to memorize the entire guide at once. Pick the phrases relevant to your current situation and start using them.

The Goethe-Institut's own A1/A2 materials take the same practical approach, emphasizing introductions, shopping, restaurants, directions and routine interactions over abstract vocabulary. See Official sources ↓.

Kategorie 2

Du or Sie?

5 phrases
SieFormal · professional · when you're unsure
Wollen wir uns duzen?
duFriends · many fellow students · once it's offered

A simple rule: when you're unsure in a formal or professional situation, Sie is the safer starting point. Use du with people you already have an informal relationship with, such as friends and many fellow students. At university and workplaces, conventions can differ: pay attention to what the other person uses.

  • Können wir uns duzen?Can we use "du"?
  • Wollen wir uns duzen?Shall we use "du"?
  • Gerne.Sure / gladly.
  • Sie können mich gerne duzen.You can use "du" with me.
  • Bleiben wir beim Sie?Shall we continue using "Sie"?
German LifeBox Tip: If you're starting a new university job, internship or professional relationship, don't assume du. Let the context guide you.

A very useful sentence

Ich lerne noch Deutsch. Könnten Sie bitte etwas langsamer sprechen?

"I'm still learning German. Could you please speak a little more slowly?"

🚫 Avoid this instead

Mein Deutsch ist schlecht.

This is much more useful than simply saying Mein Deutsch ist schlecht ("My German is bad"). Avoid unnecessarily putting yourself down: the phrase above gets the same message across while keeping the conversation constructive.

The real shortcut

Useful sentence patterns

6 patterns

Instead of memorizing 150 unrelated sentences, learn reusable patterns. One pattern can become dozens of useful sentences.

Ich hätte gerne+ noun

  • Ich hätte gerne einen Kaffee.I'd like a coffee.
  • Ich hätte gerne ein Ticket nach München.I'd like a ticket to Munich.

Wo finde ich+ noun?

  • Wo finde ich die Kasse?Where can I find the checkout?
  • Wo finde ich das Studierendensekretariat?Where can I find the student office?

Ich möchte+ infinitive/noun

  • Ich möchte einen Termin vereinbaren.I'd like to make an appointment.
  • Ich möchte ein Girokonto eröffnen.I'd like to open a current account.

Können Sie bitte+ verb?

  • Können Sie bitte warten?Could you please wait?
  • Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?Could you please repeat that?

Ich habe eine Frage zu+ noun

  • Ich habe eine Frage zu meiner Rechnung.I have a question about my bill.
  • Ich habe eine Frage zu dieser Aufgabe.I have a question about this assignment.

Ich suche+ noun

  • Ich suche eine Wohnung.I'm looking for an apartment.
  • Ich suche den Bahnhof.I'm looking for the station.

Mini pronunciation guide

8 sounds

This isn't a pronunciation course, just a handful of sounds that trip up most beginners.

  • chDifferent sounds occur after different vowels. Listen to native examples rather than treating it as one English sound.
  • zUsually sounds like ts: zehn → "tsen"
  • wSounds like English v: Wasser
  • vOften sounds like f in native German words: vier
  • jSounds like English y: ja
  • eiSounds roughly like "eye": mein
  • ieSounds like long "ee": Liebe
  • eu / äuSounds roughly like "oy": heute
Best approach: listen to native German pronunciation and imitate the rhythm rather than trying to read German using English pronunciation rules. The Goethe-Institut also offers dedicated online pronunciation training ↗ based on everyday communication.

From experience

German LifeBox tip

Don't wait until your German is perfect before speaking German. You don't need a huge vocabulary to handle everyday situations: learn a few complete phrases, use them repeatedly, and gradually swap out individual words inside the sentence.

  1. Ich hätte gerne …
  2. Ich hätte gerne einen Kaffee.
  3. Ich hätte gerne ein Ticket.
  4. Ich hätte gerne einen Termin.
  5. Ich hätte gerne die Rechnung.

One sentence pattern can become dozens of useful sentences. That's genuinely how I got through my own first months here.

📌 One timeline does not fit everyone

Your actual timeline depends on your nationality, academic background, degree level, programme, university, intake, and visa requirements. Use this planner to build your own timeline — not as a substitute for checking your university's, APS's, or your German mission's current requirements directly.

First decision

Winter or Summer?

❄️
Winter Semester
Usually begins around September/October. It's the more common intake and generally offers a broader selection of programmes.
🌱
Summer Semester
Usually begins around March/April. Not every programme admits for Summer Semester, so this should be one of your very first checks.
Important: never assume a programme is available in your preferred semester. Check the programme's official university page before building any plan around it.

Before you build a personal timeline, answer these five questions:

1
What degree are you applying for?
Bachelor's or Master's — the eligibility rules and typical timelines differ for each.
2
Which semester?
Winter or Summer — confirmed against your specific programme, not assumed.
3
Do you have direct university entrance qualification?
Direct HZB, or an indirect HZB that may need a Studienkolleg year first — see Conditional Pathways below.
4
Do you need APS?
Country- and qualification-dependent. See the APS Document Checklist for the full eligibility breakdown.
5
How will you apply?
Directly to the university, through uni-assist, through a VPD + direct application, or another university-specific procedure — this is set by each university, not by you.
Don't start with "which university is famous?" Start with "which programmes am I actually eligible for?" — this is a philosophy we mean seriously, not a throwaway line.

Avoid these

Common timeline mistakes

  • ✕Planning around the "common" deadline instead of the actual one. 15 July and 15 January are common, not universal.
  • ✕Assuming every programme starts in Winter. Always confirm Summer availability directly.
  • ✕Assuming every programme uses uni-assist. Some don't use it at all.
  • ✕Treating your uni-assist submission as the end of the VPD process. You still have to apply to the university directly with the VPD.
  • ✕Starting APS too late. Processing alone can take weeks — see the full timeline in the APS guide.
  • ✕Waiting until admission to think about finances. Start researching your financial proof route months earlier.
  • ✕Assuming visa processing takes a fixed number of weeks. It varies by mission and by season — build in buffer, not a guess.
  • ✕Booking non-refundable flights before your visa is secured. One of the most expensive mistakes on this list.
  • ✕Treating accommodation as something to solve after arrival. Start the search the moment your visa is approved, not after you land.
  • ✕Copying another student's timeline. Their nationality, programme, and university are rarely identical to yours.
💡
Bibin's Advice

When I was building my own timeline, the thing that actually saved me wasn't a fancy planner — it was one single tracking sheet with every deadline on it, reviewed every couple of weeks so nothing crept up on me.

Work backwards from your real deadline like the countdown above, not forwards from today. And don't let a "common" deadline lull you into a false sense of time — I always checked my exact programme's page directly, because the moment I assumed instead of checked was the moment I nearly missed something.

Still wondering

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing to study in Germany?+
12–15 months before your target intake is a safe starting point — it gives you room for APS (if it applies to you), language preparation, and a genuine shortlist instead of a rushed one. Starting later isn't impossible, but it removes your margin for anything going wrong.
Is one year enough to prepare?+
It can be, especially if you already meet the language requirement and don't need APS. But if APS, a language test, and document preparation all still need to happen, one year is tight rather than comfortable — start as early as you can.
What's the difference between Winter and Summer Semester?+
Winter Semester usually starts around September/October and has the broader programme selection. Summer Semester usually starts around March/April, but not every programme admits students then — always confirm on the university's own page before planning around it.
Is 15 July the deadline for all Winter Semester programmes?+
No — it's a common deadline, not a universal one. Many programmes, especially Master's courses and Studienkolleg, set their own earlier deadlines. Always confirm the specific date for your specific programme.
Is 15 January the deadline for all Summer Semester programmes?+
No, same principle as Winter — it's a common deadline that many programmes follow, but not all. Check your target programme's actual deadline directly, and confirm early that it even admits for Summer Semester at all.
Do I need APS?+
It depends on your nationality and qualification — for most Indian applicants, yes. Check the full eligibility breakdown in the APS Document Checklist rather than assuming either way.
Should I apply through uni-assist or directly?+
It depends entirely on the university — some use uni-assist for the full process, some only for a VPD before a separate direct application, and some don't use uni-assist at all. This is set by each university, not by you, so check your specific programme's application page before assuming.
What is a VPD?+
A Vorprüfungsdokumentation — a document uni-assist issues confirming what your foreign qualification corresponds to in the German system. Some universities require it before they'll consider your direct application. It's valid for one year from issue.
When should I apply for my visa?+
As soon as you have your admission offer and your financial proof arranged. Appointment availability varies by mission and by season, so book the moment you're eligible rather than waiting until closer to your intended travel date.
Can I book my flight before getting the visa?+
Don't book anything non-refundable until your visa is actually approved. It's one of the most common and most expensive timeline mistakes — treat accommodation and travel as post-visa steps, not parallel ones.
